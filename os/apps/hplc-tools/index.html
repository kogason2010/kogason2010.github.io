Equations used
  • Flow: F2 = F1 · (dc2²/dc1²) · (dp1/dp2). The dp term is optional.
  • Injection volume: V2 = V1 · (L2 dc2²)/(L1 dc1²)
  • Gradient times: t2 = t1 · (F1/F2) · (L2 dc2²)/(L1 dc1²). F2 includes any extra flow adjustment, so the gradient slope per column volume stays the same.
  • Pressure ratio (same mobile phase and temperature): P2/P1 = (F2/F1)(L2/L1)(dc1/dc2)²(dp1/dp2)²
  • Dwell: the original dwell volume is scaled to the new column volume and compared with the new system's dwell volume. A shortfall is made up with an initial isocratic hold. An excess can be offset by delaying injection, if the system supports it.

USP <621> (harmonized; official 1 Dec 2022) adjustment summary, based on USP's notice of the harmonized chapter:

  • L/dp: −25% to +50% of the original, for isocratic and gradient methods. When switching between fully and superficially porous particles, the efficiency criterion is used instead: N (isocratic) or (tR/w½)² (gradient) within −25% to +50%.
  • Flow: scaled with dc² and dp. A further ±50% is permitted.
  • Column temperature: ±10 °C (isocratic), ±5 °C (gradient). Mobile-phase pH: ±0.2 units. Minor components: ±30% relative or ±10% absolute.
  • Injection volume: may be adjusted, provided system suitability is met.

Always confirm against the current official chapter and the monograph, and verify system suitability after any change.

Equations & rules of thumb
  • k* = tG·F / (1.15·Vm·Δφ·S), with Δφ the change in organic fraction (e.g. 5→95% gives 0.90) and Vm = ε·π(dc/2)²·L.
  • k* between about 2 and 10 is the usual working range; a value near 5 is a common target. To keep the separation unchanged when you change column, flow or gradient range, keep k* constant.
  • S ≈ 4 is typical for small molecules (MW below ~500). It is much larger for peptides and proteins, so their retention is far more sensitive to %B.
  • Peak capacity nc = 1 + tG/w, with w the average 4σ (baseline) peak width.
  • Scouting rule of thumb (Snyder & Dolan): ΔtR/tG < 0.25 suggests isocratic elution is feasible; > 0.40 suggests gradient elution; in between, either may work.

Equations used
  • k = (tR − t0)/t0; α = k2/k1
  • N = 5.54 (tR/w½)² or N = 16 (tR/wb)²; reduced plate height h = (L/N)/dp. A well-packed column gives h ≈ 2–3.
  • Rs = 1.18 (tR2 − tR1)/(w½1 + w½2) or Rs = 2 (tR2 − tR1)/(wb1 + wb2)
  • Symmetry factor As = W0.05/(2f). Ph. Eur. 2.2.46 applies a default of 0.8–1.8 unless the monograph states otherwise. USP monographs set their own limits (often NMT 2.0).
  • S/N = 2H/h, with h the peak-to-peak noise range measured in a blank over at least 5× the peak width at half height (USP <621>). The LOD and LOQ estimates assume a linear response through zero: c × 3/(S/N) and c × 10/(S/N).
  • %RSD = 100 · s/x̄, with the sample standard deviation s (n − 1).
  • Purnell: Rs = (√N/4) · ((α−1)/α) · (k/(1+k)); required N = 16 Rs² (α/(α−1))² ((1+k)/k)²

pH of an acid additive in water

Method, pKa values and reagent data
  • Every pair here differs by one proton, so the charge balance gives nbase = C·α − [H⁺] + [OH⁻] and nacid = C − nbase, with α the fraction in the base form. The [H⁺] term matters below about pH 3. There, plain Henderson–Hasselbalch overstates the base form (e.g. KH₂PO₄ at pH 2.5).
  • Activity: the pH-meter reading is treated as −log aH⁺. The effective pKa′ = pKa + A·(2za − 1)·(√I/(1+√I) − 0.3I), with A = 0.509 (25 °C) and za the charge of the acid form, iterated on ionic strength I. The Davies equation is reasonable up to I ≈ 0.1–0.5 M.
  • Organic solvent shifts both the pKa and the measured pH; these values apply to the aqueous buffer.

Equations & limits
  • ΔP = φ · η · L · u0/dp², with u0 = F/(ε·π dc²/4), the chromatographic linear velocity (L/t0). φ is commonly quoted as roughly 500–1000 for packed columns. It varies with packing quality and particle type, so for the best estimate, enter a measured pressure and the tool back-calculates φ for your column.
  • Viscosity: Chen & Horváth (1993) correlation, ln η = φ(a₁ + b₁/T) + (1−φ)(a₂ + b₂/T) + φ(1−φ)(a₃ + b₃/T), with φ the organic volume fraction and T in kelvin. It reproduces the pure-solvent viscosities and the methanol–water maximum (≈1.6 cP near 40–50% at 25 °C). Mid-range values are approximate (±10–15%). It ignores buffer salts and additives.
  • System pressure (tubing, mixer, detector) is not included.
